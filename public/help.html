1 · Find it

Your free scan and report

What does the scan check?

HindSited opens your website the way a customer does, on a real phone and a computer, and reads up to 25 of your pages. It looks for:

  • Phone problems: text that's too small, buttons too close together, pages that slide sideways.
  • Ways to reach you: phone, email, a contact form, and an easy way to get a price.
  • Trust: reviews on your homepage, the security padlock, a real address.
  • Google basics: page titles, descriptions, a sitemap.
  • Speed: Google's own speed test.
  • Mistakes: broken links, template text, fake phone numbers, $0 prices, old dates.

It only reads public pages. It never logs in, fills in forms or changes anything.

Why are some issues blurred?

A free report shows about 60% of the issues found: the smaller ones. The most serious 40% are blurred, with a count of how many there are and which areas they're in.

Subscribe (Solo or Agency) and the same report unlocks straight away: every issue, where it is on your page, what it's likely costing you, and how to fix it.

How many free scans do I get?
  • One free scan per website, ever. That stops the same site being scanned free over and over.
  • One free scan per account.
  • A daily limit per internet connection, to stop abuse.

Paid plans include 30 checks a month (Solo) or 300 (Agency), so you can re-check after every change.

Who can see my report?

Anyone you send the report's link to. Nobody else can find it: it isn't listed anywhere or shown to search engines.

2 · Fix it

Fixing your current website

Two ways: fix the issues yourself with the steps in your report, or get a brand-new website built.

Will you fix my existing website for me?

No. We build new websites only. Fixing someone else's site means logging into their Wix, Squarespace, WordPress or Shopify account and working around how it was built, which is slow and risky for everyone.

You have two options:

  1. Fix it yourself. Every issue in your report comes with plain steps (below). You can also send the report link to whoever manages your site.
  2. Get a new website with every issue gone, from $500 CAD. See Your new website.
How do I fix the issues myself?
  1. Open your report. Each issue shows what's wrong, what it's likely costing you, how to fix it, and a screenshot of where it is on your page.
  2. Start at the top. Issues are ranked by how much they're likely costing you.
  3. Make the change in your website builder (Wix, Squarespace, WordPress and so on).
  4. Run a new check from your dashboard and watch your score go up.

Two of the most common gaps have a tool that fixes them for you: Review Booster (no reviews) and Quote Calculator (no easy way to get a price).

I don't know who built my site or how to log in

Check your email and your bank or card statements for a monthly or yearly charge from a website company (Wix, Squarespace, Shopify, GoDaddy, WordPress.com, a web host). Whoever charges you is where your site lives, and their log-in page has a Forgot password link.

The About your website section of your report shows what your site is built on and where your web address is registered.

If you can't get in at all, your site isn't really in your control. A new website you own fixes that for good: see Your new website.

Quote Calculator

An instant price on your website. Visitors pick what they need, see a price straight away, and send you their details.

What is it, and why does it work?

Most people won't call a business just to ask a price. They leave and try the next site, the one that shows a number. The Quote Calculator gives them that number straight away, so they stay and send you their details.

  • You list your services and prices (a business type fills in examples to start).
  • Optional: a quantity (rooms, hours, square feet) and add-ons.
  • You choose what's shown: an exact price, a range (for example $180 to $220), or price on request (no price shown, details still collected).
What does my customer see and do?
  1. They pick a service, set the quantity and tick any extras.
  2. They see the price straight away.
  3. They leave their name and an email or phone number to get the quote.
  4. They see a thank-you message saying you'll be in touch.
What do I get?
  • An email the moment someone asks, with their contact details, exactly what they picked, and the price they saw.
  • Every request saved in your dashboard, where you can download them.
  • Reply fast: people who hear back within the hour are far more likely to book.
Where does it live? Does it go on my website?

Your calculator lives on HindSited at its own link (for example hindsighted.netlify.app/q/your-business). There are three ways to put it in front of customers. Pick whichever is easiest:

  1. A button on your website (works everywhere, no code). Add a button to your site that says something like "Get an instant price" and link it to your calculator's link. Every website builder can add a button with a link.
  2. Built into a page (embed). Paste one line of code into your site and the calculator appears right on the page. Step-by-step for your platform is below.
  3. Share the link. Put it on your Google Business Profile, Facebook, Instagram bio, text replies and email signature. No website needed.

Your link and your embed code are on your Quote Calculator page, each with a Copy button.

Step by step: put the calculator on my website

Easiest, works on every platform: add a Button to your page, set its text to "Get an instant price", and paste your calculator's link as the button's link. Publish.

To show it right on the page, copy your embed code from your Quote Calculator page, then follow your platform:

Wix
  1. Open your site in the Wix Editor.
  2. Click Add Elements (the + on the left), then Embed Code, then Popular Embeds, then Embed HTML.
  3. Click the new box on your page, then Enter Code.
  4. Paste your code and click Apply.
  5. Drag the box where you want it, make it wide enough, then Publish.
Squarespace
  1. Open the page and click Edit.
  2. Click Add Block where you want it and choose Code.
  3. Click the block's pencil icon and paste your code.
  4. Click Save.

Some Squarespace plans don't run code in code blocks. If the calculator doesn't appear on your live site, use the button method instead.

WordPress
  1. Edit the page.
  2. Click the + (block inserter) and search for Custom HTML.
  3. Paste your code into the block.
  4. Click Update (or Publish).

On WordPress.com, adding code needs a plan that allows it. If it doesn't show, use the button method.

Shopify
  1. In Shopify admin go to Online Store, then Themes, then Customize.
  2. Open the page you want, click Add section and choose Custom Liquid.
  3. Paste your code into the Custom Liquid box.
  4. Click Save.

Most current Shopify themes include Custom Liquid. If yours doesn't, use the button method.

GoDaddy
  1. Open your Websites + Marketing site and click Edit Website.
  2. Click Add Section, search for HTML, and add it.
  3. Paste your code into the Custom Code box.
  4. Click Publish.
Webflow
  1. In the Designer, open the Add panel and drag a Code Embed onto the page.
  2. Paste your code and click Save & Close.
  3. Publish the site. (It shows as a placeholder in the Designer and appears on the live site.)

Webflow needs a paid site or workspace plan for code embeds. If you don't have one, use the button method.

Web address

Your web address (domain)

Your web address (like yourbusiness.com) is rented yearly from a domain company, called a registrar. Your new site keeps your address. You just point it at the new site.

How do I find where my web address is registered?
  1. Check your HindSited report. Under About your website it shows who your web address is registered with and when it renews.
  2. Search your email for "domain renewal", "domain registration" or your web address.
  3. Check your bank or card statements for a yearly charge from GoDaddy, Namecheap, Squarespace, Wix, Google, Hover, IONOS or similar.
  4. Look it up at lookup.icann.org: type your web address and it shows the registrar.
I can log in to my domain account

Then you're ready. At launch, follow The two settings. It takes about 5 minutes.

I can't log in, or I forgot my password
  1. Go to your registrar's log-in page and click Forgot password.
  2. Try every email your business has used, including old ones and a past employee's.
  3. Still stuck? Contact the registrar's support. They can confirm you're the owner with ID and business documents.

You can still send your new-website request while you sort this out. Building takes 1 to 2 weeks, which is usually plenty of time.

Someone else (a past designer) registered it

Ask them to transfer the domain into an account in your name. It's free or cheap, and they don't lose anything. If you can't reach them, contact the registrar: they have a process for business owners to prove the address is theirs (business registration, invoices, ID).

I don't have a web address, or I can't get mine back

No problem:

  1. Your new site can go live straight away on a free temporary address (like yourbusiness.netlify.app), so you can start sharing it.
  2. Buy a web address in your own name from any registrar (Namecheap, GoDaddy, Cloudflare and others). It costs about $15 to $25 a year. We'll help you pick a good one.
  3. Then do the two settings and your new address opens your site.
The two settings (exact values)

When your site is ready, Michael emails you the exact values. They look like this:

  1. Log in to your domain company and open your web address's DNS settings. It's often called DNS, Manage DNS, DNS records or Advanced DNS.
  2. Setting 1: your main address. Make sure there's one record with
    Type: A · Host/Name: @ (or leave it blank) · Value/Points to: 75.2.60.5
    If your provider offers an ALIAS or ANAME type, you can use that instead, with the value apex-loadbalancer.netlify.com.
  3. Setting 2: the "www" version. Make sure there's one record with
    Type: CNAME · Host/Name: www · Value/Points to: your site's address from Michael's email (it ends in .netlify.app)
  4. Remove the old ones. Delete any other A, AAAA or CNAME records on @ or www that point to your old site. Leave everything else alone.
  5. Save. It can take a few hours, and up to a day, to switch over. The padlock (https) turns on by itself once it does.

Don't touch your email settings. Leave any MX or TXT records exactly as they are, so your business email keeps working.

Stuck? Reply to Michael's email with a screenshot of your DNS page and he'll tell you exactly what to change.
